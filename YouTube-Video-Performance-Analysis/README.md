# YouTube Video Performance Analysis

## Overview

This project examines YouTube video performance using data collected from my own channel. The analysis explores how upload timing relates to video performance, using average and median views per day to compare different upload periods.

## Methodology & Tools

- **Data Preparation (Excel):** Organized video data and calculated video age, views per day, and upload time categories.
- **SQL Analysis (SQLite):** Grouped videos by upload period, calculated average views per day, and examined upload distribution.
- **Data Validation (Power Query):** Inspected data quality, filtered incomplete records, and verified data types.
- **Visualization (Power BI):** Compared average and median views per day across upload periods and examined performance by upload hour.

## Key Findings

- **Evening uploads** had the highest average views per day at 1.92, compared with 1.36 for night uploads and 0.79 for afternoon uploads.
- **Night uploads** had the highest median views per day, suggesting that typical performance differed from what the averages alone indicated.
- Evening uploads represented the largest share of the dataset, with 101 videos, compared with 45 afternoon uploads and 41 night uploads.
- A small number of high-performing videos influenced the average views per day, particularly for evening uploads.
- The hourly analysis showed variation in average performance across individual upload hours.

## Conclusion

Evening uploads had the highest average views per day, while night uploads had the highest median. This difference highlights how unusually high-performing videos can influence averages and why examining both measures provides a more complete picture of performance.

These findings suggest that upload timing may be associated with differences in video performance, although the analysis does not establish a causal relationship.
